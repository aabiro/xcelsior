## The problem

We depend on people lending us their GPUs. We had never told them, straight out:

1. **What we expect** — how much availability makes a good provider.
2. **How scoring actually works** — the reputation engine was real, but the public
   docs (`trust.mdx`) described a *different, older* model (a 0–100 scale with
   Bronze→Platinum) than the code implements (a 0–850+ scale, New→Diamond, with a
   reliability *multiplier*). We were misrepresenting our own system.
3. **What reliability is worth** — and, critically, that **dropping a job mid-run
   hurts the whole network**, not just the one customer.

We also wanted concrete, motivating goals ("hit X hours → earn more") **without
building an incentive that bankrupts us.**

## The key insight: the incentives are already self-funding

The single most important design fact — and the reason this can't blow up the
budget — is that **every reward a provider unlocks is self-funding**. Reading
`reputation.py`:

- **Lower platform commission** (15% → 8% across tiers) is only reached by driving
  reliable volume. 8% of a lot beats 15% of a little; the extra share comes out of
  margin we would never have captured without that reliability.
- **Search boost** (0.8× → 2.0×) is *allocation*, not cash — reliable hosts get
  matched first, earning more billable hours at no cost to us.
- **Price premium** (0% → 50%) is paid by **buyers**, not by us.
- The **reliability multiplier** means an unreliable host's whole score scales
  *down* — the system self-corrects without us paying anyone to behave.

So we deliberately did **not** add a flat "cash bonus for X hours." "Earn more"
always means "you became more valuable to the network." That's the honest message
*and* the safe one — they're the same message.

## The concrete numbers we now communicate

All derived in `provider_expectations.py` directly from the engine constants, so
they can never drift from what the code does (a test enforces this).

### Weekly uptime goals (the behavioural target)

| Target | Hours/week | What it gets you |
|--------|-----------|------------------|
| Casual | 20 | Stay listed, outrun activity decay |
| **Reliable (recommended)** | **40** | Strong reliability score, steady climb to Gold |
| Pro | 100+ | Fastest path to Platinum/Diamond, 90–92% payout band |

We explicitly tell providers **we are not asking for 24/7** — predictable
part-time beats erratic full-time.

### What climbing is worth (self-funding)

| Tier | Min score | Our cut | You keep | Premium | Search |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|---------|--------|
| New | 0 | 15% | 85% | — | 0.8× |
| Bronze | 100 | 15% | 85% | — | 1.0× |
| Silver | 250 | 15% | 85% | +5% | 1.1× |
| Gold | 450 | 12% | 88% | +20% | 1.25× |
| Platinum | 650 | 10% | 90% | +40% | 1.5× |
| Diamond | 850 | 8% | 92% | +50% | 2.0× |

Headline we lead with: **a Gold host keeps ~3% more of every dollar and ranks 25%
higher; top hosts keep 92%.**

### The reliability message ("don't hurt everyone")

Dropping a job mid-run triggers a **double hit**: a −75 SLA-breach penalty **and** a
fall in the reliability multiplier that scales the entire score down — one dropped
week can cost a tier. We pair this with the release valve: **planned downtime is
free** — stop accepting new jobs before going offline and there's no penalty.

## Where the message lives (three reinforcing surfaces)

1. **Signup email campaign** (`routes/auth.py`) — providers now get a role-aware
   welcome email that lays out the goal, the 3-line scoring model, the payoff, and
   the reliability warning. Copy is generated by `provider_welcome_email_text()`.
   Providers who sign up via OAuth (no verification email) get the same content as
   an inbox notification via `provider_welcome_notification()`.
2. **The site / docs** — new canonical [`Earn as a Provider`](../fern/pages/earn.mdx)
   page (published at `https://docs.xcelsior.ca/earn`), plus a corrected
   `Trust & Reputation` page whose tables now match the engine. The dashboard
   **Reputation** page already renders the live tier/perk view.
3. **Onboarding wizard** (`wizard/src/learn-content.ts`) — two new learn slides
   ("Aim for ~40 hours a week", "Don't vanish mid-job") reinforce the message at
   the moment someone sets up as a provider.

## Why it stays honest: one source of truth

`provider_expectations.py` imports the real constants from `reputation.py` and
derives every human-facing number. `tests/test_provider_expectations.py` fails if
the email, the tables, or the penalties ever disagree with the engine. Change an
incentive in `reputation.py` and every surface updates with it — or the test goes
red. This is the mechanism that prevents the exact drift we just fixed in
`trust.mdx`.
